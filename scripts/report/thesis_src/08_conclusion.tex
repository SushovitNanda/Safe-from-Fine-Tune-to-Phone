\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We followed one small multilingual model from benign fine-tuning to a quantized deployment. Fine-tuning on medical exam questions doubled attack success. About half of the unsafe labels fall on degenerate text, and on non-degenerate answers the rise is still 10.9 points. A steering vector added at run time removed about half of the all-prompt increase, and about three quarters of the non-degenerate rise, without measurable loss of medical accuracy. The repair kept a measurable effect at 4-bit NF4, in Q4\_K\_M and Q3\_K\_M files, without the preamble, and in a simulated budget configuration on another machine and engine. The chain broke in the middle in our hands: we could not link the circuit map to the drift, and restricting a weight-space repair to that map did not help here. Spending extra bits on the map's tensors protected the repair no better than spending them on random tensors, though stock Q3\_K\_M had already kept it. The safety story for Hinglish is clouded by a medical model that mostly cannot write it. We are confident in the pooled and English drift, in the cross-machine agreement, and in steering as a partial repair that survives deployment. We are not confident in any mechanistic explanation, or in Hinglish magnitudes.

\section{Future Work}\label{sec:future}
\paragraph{From what we failed to achieve.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Weight-space repair.} ReSta's global safety vector appears to carry capability with it. Future work should project the refusal direction out of the weight update, or restrict the patch to directions orthogonal to task performance, rather than to a node list.
\item \textbf{Circuit discovery.} We could not explain a behavioural change with a circuit that did not change beyond its noise floor; a different attribution target might. Better options are attribution against the \emph{drift} itself (medical minus original activations), larger pair sets for Hinglish, unsigned or split-sign scoring so that the necessity test is monotone, and discovery with the preamble on hardware with more memory.
\item \textbf{Hinglish.} The medical model degenerates in Hinglish. Fine-tuning data in the target language and register, or a decoding-time repetition guard, should come before any Hinglish safety claim. Judges also need a Hinglish refusal validation set.
\item \textbf{The selection rule.} A 5-point over-refusal limit on 20 to 30 items is one refusal. Future pre-registrations should size dev sets for the rule and define the fallback.
\item \textbf{Over-refusal cost of steering.} Strength could be scheduled per language, or the vector applied only when a harm classifier fires, to reduce the clinical over-refusal cost.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{To extend what worked.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Repeat the drift measurement over several fine-tuning seeds and datasets, and on other small models.
\item Test the steering vector against adaptive and multi-turn attacks, and on phone hardware rather than a simulated configuration.
\item Repeat the protection comparison with several random draws, so that tensor-selection variance is part of the interval; the one draw we ran moved safety between English and Hinglish.
\item Replace the AI readings with human labels from native speakers with clinical training, including a human intra-rater check in place of the AI re-label.
\item Measure deployment cost on workloads that stop at the end of a turn.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{With more credits: planned approach and expected before and after}
All reported work ran on one 12\,GB laptop GPU and one 24\,GB Mac; no cloud credits were used beyond one parity check on a free Colab T4. With a 40 to 80\,GB GPU for a few days we would do five things, in this order. (1) Repeat the two fine-tunes over five training seeds and two further medical sets, because every drift and repair interval here resamples prompts, not training runs; the present drift interval (Section~\ref{sec:rq2}) comes from one run, and the result would be a between-seed interval around it. (2) Run EAP-IG with the preamble on, which does not fit the 12\,GB laptop GPU, and attribute against the drift itself (medical minus original activations); the test of success is a map whose restricted repair moves pooled attack success by at least the 5-point margin, against no measurable effect now (Section~\ref{sec:rq4}). (3) Repeat the protection comparison over ten random tensor draws instead of one, so that tensor-selection variance enters the interval; the present pooled difference is zero (Section~\ref{sec:rq6}). (4) Replace the AI readings with native-speaker, clinically trained human labels on the 300-item sample and add a Hinglish refusal validation set, since Hinglish refusal cannot be judged reliably (Section~\ref{sec:judges}). (5) Fine-tune with Hinglish-register data or add a decoding-time repetition guard and re-measure, because most of the medical model's Hinglish answers to harmful prompts are degenerate (Section~\ref{sec:valres}) and its measured Hinglish rate is an upper bound. Expected before and after, stated as hypotheses: (1) drift stays within about 5 points of $+18.1$ across seeds; (3) protected minus random stays within $\pm 3$ points of zero over ten draws; (2) would count as success only if a restricted repair reached the 5-point margin, against $-0.6$ now; (4) human-vs-approved $\kappa$ above 0.6 in every language; (5) the Hinglish degenerate share falling from 64.7\,\% to below 20\,\%, which would lower the measured Hinglish rate of 62.7\,\% toward the non-degenerate estimate. We do not predict the outcome of (2), because the circuit link failed twice in this project.
