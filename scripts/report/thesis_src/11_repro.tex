\section{Reproduction Details}\label{app:repro}
This appendix records every setting a reader needs to repeat the pipeline; the submission repository's documentation gives the commands.

\subsection{Models and pinned snapshots}
Base model: Tiny Aya Global (Cohere Labs), pinned to one Hub snapshot on both machines. ReSta reference pair: Tiny Aya Global and Tiny Aya Base at pinned snapshots. Judges: Qwen3Guard-Gen-4B and WildGuard at pinned snapshots. Translation: IndicTrans2 Indic-to-English 1B for back-translation and English-to-Indic 1B for the Hindi drafts. The laptop lane did not pass a revision at training or merge time; the snapshots used are the ones resolved in the local cache, recorded in the repository, and the Mac lane pinned the same Tiny Aya Global snapshot explicitly.

\subsection{Data pipeline (CuratorKIT)}
Fine-tuning sets were curated with CuratorKIT 0.1.21: MinHash deduplication (3-gram shingles, 128 permutations, Jaccard $\ge 0.85$), toxicity gate on with Detoxify's unbiased model, 10 to 4,096 tokens, ShareGPT export. The MedMCQA SFT rows keep single-choice questions with explanations of at least 60 characters and render the answer as the answer letter followed by the explanation; rows over 1,024 templated tokens are dropped and the set is stratified by subject. The control set is trimmed at random (seed 43) until the two token totals agree within 1\,\%. Leak checks use exact matching on NFKC lower-cased text, the same MinHash at $\ge 0.85$, and cosine $\ge 0.92$ of multilingual MiniLM-L12 paraphrase embeddings. Hindi drafts of XSTest and of the harmless calibration prompts come from IndicTrans2 English-to-Indic (5 beams, 256 tokens) and were post-edited. Calibration pairs: 100 Aya~EN against 100 Alpaca prompts (English-only) and 33 EN + 33 HI + 33 Hinglish per side (three-language), drawn stratified by category with seeds 23 to 27, none from any test set. The release is CC-BY-NC-4.0; sources are Aya Red-teaming (Apache-2.0), XSTest (CC-BY-4.0), MedMCQA (Apache-2.0) and Alpaca-cleaned (CC-BY-NC-4.0).

\subsection{Fine-tuning and merging (PEFT, Transformers)}
LoRA rank 16, $\alpha = 32$, dropout 0.05 on all linear modules (PEFT 0.21.2), trained with a Transformers 5.22.1 Trainer subclass; per-device batch 1 with 16 accumulation steps; AdamW, learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, cosine schedule, 3\,\% warm-up, gradient clipping 1.0; one epoch; FP16 mixed precision; gradient checkpointing; seed 13; prompt tokens masked out so the loss covers assistant tokens only, with the language-model head applied only at label positions. Training took 38 min (medical, peak 9.3\,GB) and 40 min (control, peak 8.9\,GB) on the laptop. Merging folds the adapter into an FP32 CPU copy and saves FP16 safetensors in 5\,GB shards with per-shard SHA-256 (28\,s); a streaming variant computes the same arithmetic one tensor at a time and writes the per-tensor SHA-256 manifest used for the weight-parity check.

\subsection{Generation}
Laptop: greedy decoding, 256 new tokens, batch 16 with left padding (halved on out-of-memory), torch seed 13, stop ids $\{3\}$ before Amendment~2 and $\{3, 6\}$ after. NF4 uses bitsandbytes 4-bit NormalFloat with FP16 compute and double quantization. Mac: the llama.cpp server at the pinned build with all layers on the GPU, eight parallel slots, a 16,384-token context, flash attention on and Jinja templates; FP16 key-value cache (Q4\_0 for the budget-phone stack); an empty chat template for no-preamble cells; the end-of-turn token (id 6) set as the stop token; and, for steered cells, the control vector applied at scale 0.5 on layer 24 only. Prompts are rendered locally, checked against the template stored in the GGUF, and sent as token ids to the completions endpoint with prompt caching off, temperature 0, seed 13, 256 tokens, eight concurrent workers. GGUFs come from llama.cpp's converter at F16 followed by its quantizer to Q4\_K\_M and Q3\_K\_M without an importance matrix.

\subsection{Judging}
Qwen3Guard-Gen-4B runs in FP16 with its own chat template over the (prompt, answer) pair, greedy, 32 new tokens (retried once at 64), batch 8; labels are parsed from its safety line (Safe, Unsafe or Controversial) and its refusal line (Yes or No), and unparseable outputs become unsure. WildGuard runs in 8-bit (bitsandbytes) with its official instruction template, greedy, 32 new tokens, batch 4, parsed from its harmful-request, response-refusal and harmful-response lines. Back-translation splits Hindi text into sentences and translates Devanagari Hindi to English with two beams, 96 new tokens and 256-token inputs, FP16, batch 16, key-value cache off; mixed Latin--Devanagari Hinglish is passed as Hindi. It runs in a side environment (Python 3.13, transformers 4.57.6, IndicTransToolkit 1.1.1) because the model's remote code needs an older transformers interface. Cohen's $\kappa$ is computed on two binary tasks: unsafe on harmful prompts (approved label unsafe) and refusal on all prompts (approved label refusal or over-refusal), with unsure items dropped.

\subsection{Circuits (CircuitKIT)}
CircuitKIT 0.2.0 (TransformerLens 3.8.0) discovery with EAP-IG at node level, 3 integration steps, sparsity 0.3, batch size 1, bfloat16 weights, template off. Split halves use a seeded random split (seed 13). Full runs take 19 to 30 min and half runs 12 to 18 min on the laptop. Validation uses 100 harmful development prompts per language (Aya dev plus the ClinicSafe dev harmful side) with the preamble on, seed 13, five random node sets disjoint from the top-$k$, attention heads handled in $z$-space (per-head outputs before the output projection) and MLPs on their outputs; necessity mean-ablates at every position, sufficiency patches at the decision position only. MLP 0 is excluded from every ranking because tied-embedding attribution inflates it. The tensor map ranks GGUF tensors by the summed absolute node score of the original model's three language runs (each normalised to sum one) and keeps the top 5, 10 and 20\,\% lists (12, 25 and 50 of 249 tensors).

\subsection{Repairs (SafeTune reference, own code)}
ReSta: $\theta_{\mathrm{rep}} = \theta_{\mathrm{med}} + \alpha\,(\theta_{\mathrm{Global}} - \theta_{\mathrm{Base}})$ over all 2-D weights (or the restricted list), FP32 arithmetic, FP16 save, verified against SafeTune 0.1.8's ReSta wrapper with DARE off on sampled tensors. Steering: $v_\ell = \overline{h_\ell}(\text{harmful}) - \overline{h_\ell}(\text{harmless})$ at the last prompt token of the official template, FP16 forward passes, float64 accumulation, no normalisation; applied as $h_\ell \leftarrow h_\ell + s\,v_\ell$ at every position after decoder layer $\ell$. The export writes the vector as a GGUF control vector with one FP32 tensor for layer 24 and a layer count of 1.

\subsection{Protection (llama.cpp tensor types)}
The protection build takes the stock Q3\_K\_M inventory, upgrades the eight highest-ranked eligible tensors to Q8\_0, downgrades the lowest-ranked eligible tensors to Q2\_K in rank order until the predicted size (GGML block sizes, 32-byte alignment) is at or below stock, and rejects any build outside a 1\,\% size tolerance. The random control draws tensors of the same (shape, type) class with seed 13 and must match the predicted size. Layer-0 feed-forward tensors are excluded from all three roles. Rules are written as per-tensor type rules for llama.cpp's quantizer. Verification checks the end-of-sequence id and chat-template hash against stock, every tensor's type against the rule, actual size against the prediction, and SHA-256 byte identity of every unselected tensor.

\subsection{Statistics (AuditKIT and own code)}
10,000 paired bootstrap resamples with NumPy's default generator seeded 13, percentile intervals at 2.5 and 97.5, two-sided $p$ as twice the smaller tail with a floor of $10^{-4}$; retention intervals use only resamples with a positive denominator; exact McNemar from statsmodels; Holm over the pre-registered family and a separate deployment family (extended by RQ6); the 58-test union is the second author's computation, reported as a sensitivity.

\subsection{Environment and compute}
Laptop: Windows 11 (10.0.26300), NVIDIA RTX 4080 Laptop GPU 12\,GB (driver 595.79), 32\,GB RAM, Python 3.12.10, torch 2.11.0+cu128, transformers 5.22.1, peft 0.21.2, bitsandbytes 0.50.2, accelerate 1.15.0, datasets 5.1.0, safetensors 0.8.0, transformer-lens 3.8.0, auditkit 1.1.3, gguf 0.19.0, sentence-transformers 6.2.0, detoxify 0.5.4 (pinned in the repository). Mac: Apple M5, 24\,GB, macOS 27.0.1, Python 3.12.15, torch 2.11.0, llama.cpp at a pinned build with embedded Metal (AppleClang 21, MacOSX 26.5 SDK), dependencies pinned in the repository. Approximate stage times: fine-tuning 38 and 40 min; merge under a minute; one FP16 test cell 34 to 38 min and one NF4 cell 61 to 63 min when answers ran to the cap, 25 to 27 min after the end-of-turn stop; one dev pass about 5 min; one EAP-IG run 19 to 30 min and twelve split halves about 3 h; Qwen3Guard judging about 8 to 12 min per cell and WildGuard with back-translation about 15 min per cell; Mac deployment cells 35 to 64 min each. The laptop lane used roughly 35 to 40 GPU-hours in total and the Mac lane roughly 10 hours of Metal inference; neither figure was logged as a single number and both are reconstructed from the per-cell run records and the decision log.
